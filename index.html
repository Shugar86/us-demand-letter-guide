US demand letter guide

How to write a demand letter that gets a clear answer

A demand letter puts a dispute in writing: what happened, what you want, by when, and what you will do next. It is often the first practical step before a payment dispute, an agency complaint or small claims court.

The six parts every demand letter needs

  1. The facts, in order. Who, what, when and how much. Dates and amounts matter more than adjectives.
  2. One specific request. A refund, a repair, the return of a deposit, unpaid wages, or a stop to billing. Several vague requests are easier to ignore than one clear one.
  3. The amount and how you got it. Show the calculation: hours times rate, the deposit minus documented deductions, the charges after cancellation.
  4. A response date. A reasonable period such as 7 to 14 days, counted from receipt.
  5. The next step. A payment dispute, a complaint to the right agency, or small claims court. Name only steps you are prepared to take.
  6. Your evidence. List the receipts, contracts, photos, statements and messages you can provide.

Requested deadlines versus legal deadlines

The date in your letter is a request. Some situations also have deadlines set by law, and missing them can cost you protection. A few US examples:

SituationDeadline that matters
Credit card billing errorYour written notice generally must reach the card issuer within 60 days after it sent the statement showing the charge (Regulation Z).
Debt collector letter you disputeA written validation dispute is generally due within the 30-day validation period (FDCPA).
Security depositSet by state law, for example 21 days in California, 14 days in New York and 30 days in Texas after you move out.
Final paycheckSet by state law, for example immediately on discharge in California and within six days after discharge in Texas.
Medical records requestA HIPAA-covered provider generally must act within 30 days, with one possible 30-day extension.
Marketing emails after you unsubscribedOpt-out requests must be honored within 10 business days (CAN-SPAM).

Do not let a reply date in your own letter run past a legal deadline. If one is close, act on it in parallel.

How to send it with proof

  • Use the notice address in the contract or lease, or the address the business gave you in writing.
  • Certified Mail gives a mailing and delivery record; add Return Receipt for a signature.
  • An email copy is useful, but it should not replace a notice method the contract requires.
  • Keep a copy of the letter, the attachments and the proof of sending together.

What a demand letter cannot do

It does not file a case, it is not a court order, and it does not guarantee payment. Its value is a clear, dated record of your request that a business, an agency or a judge can read quickly. Avoid threats you will not follow through on and never present penalties as already owed.
